What it actually does

Every claim here is something the code does and the record can be checked against.

◎

Screens before it trades

Ranks every active market on the venue by 24-hour turnover, flags listings too young to have measurable history, and attaches per-coin news — so the day starts from what is liquid and moving, not from a list written months ago.

⚖

Sizes by risk, not by feel

Every position is sized so a stop-out costs the same fraction of the account. Portfolio heat, correlation and net beta are capped per risk factor, and beta is measured from the tape rather than assumed.

↗

Presses winners, books profit

Adds units to positions that are working, each risking less than the last, and sells slices at set multiples of risk so a trend does not hand its gains back while the trailing stop catches up.

📰

Reads the wire

Per-instrument sentiment from editorial and exchange feeds, weighted by coverage and recency. A single excitable headline cannot open a position, and good news in a downtrend is refused.

🛑

Knows when to stop

A daily loss limit, a drawdown halt, a cooldown after losing streaks and a hard cap on new positions per day. The bot is allowed to decide it should not trade today.

🔍

Reports against itself

Every expectancy carries its standard error and the sample it would need to be real. Results that are not statistically distinguishable from zero are labelled as such rather than advertised.

Honest about what this is

This runs on paper by default. The performance shown on this page is a simulation over historical bars, with modelled fees, slippage and funding — not a live account. Backtests flatter: they fill at prices nobody queued for, they know which markets survived, and a single run cannot separate an edge from a lucky path. Numbers here are reported with their error bars for that reason. Nothing on this page is financial advice, and trading carries the risk of losing everything committed to it.
